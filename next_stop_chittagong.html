Street Legends & Port City Feasts

Fuchka

Crispy hollow spheres of pure joy, filled with spiced mashed potato, chickpeas and a splash of fiery tamarind water that explodes on your tongue. One bite and the streets of Chittagong roar to life. This is the original street-side adrenaline shot — tangy, spicy, crunchy, addictive.

Doi Fuchka

The royal cousin of classic fuchka. Cool, creamy yogurt cascades over the crispy shells like a golden fountain of tok (sweet-tangy sauce), meeting spicy potato filling and a final shower of chaat masala. Every bite is a cooling explosion after the heat — pure Bangladeshi street poetry in edible form.

Shingara

Golden, flaky, pyramid-shaped perfection. Thin, buttery pastry hugging a fragrant filling of spiced potato, cauliflower and peas. Served piping hot with a sharp tomato-chili chutney. The ultimate tea-time companion that makes rainy Chittagong afternoons unforgettable.

Shingara Chaat

Crushed shingara meets cool yogurt, tangy tamarind, fiery green chutney, onions, coriander and a blizzard of sev. A deconstructed masterpiece that dances between crunch, creaminess and heat. Street food elevated to an art form — messy, loud and completely irresistible.

Chicken Samosa

Juicy minced chicken seasoned with garam masala, green chili and coriander, wrapped in a shatteringly crisp pastry triangle. Each bite releases a wave of aromatic steam. The meaty answer to every vegetarian snack craving — bold, satisfying and dangerously moreish.

Daal Puri

Soft, puffed puris stuffed with spiced red lentil paste, deep-fried until golden and blistered. Tear one open and the earthy, fragrant daal aroma hits you first. Perfect with a squeeze of lemon or a side of cucumber salad — pure comfort from the heart of Bangladesh.

Khichuri with Mejbani Gorur Mangsho

The soul of Chittagong hospitality. Soft, ghee-laced rice-and-lentil khichuri served alongside legendary Mejbani beef — slow-cooked, deeply spiced, fall-apart tender meat that melts into the rice. This is the feast that brings entire neighborhoods together. One plate and you understand why Chittagong people are famous for their big hearts.

Chapli Kabaab with Puri

Flat, crispy-edged, juicy minced meat patties loaded with crushed coriander, pomegranate seeds, tomato and green chili. Served sizzling hot with puffy golden puris. The smoky, spicy aroma alone is enough to stop traffic. A Pashtun classic that found a second home in the port city.

Steam Momos

Delicate, hand-pleated dumplings filled with juicy minced chicken or vegetables, steamed until the wrappers turn translucent and the filling bursts with ginger-garlic heat. Served with fiery tomato-chili achar. Soft, steaming, addictive little pockets of Himalayan comfort that have conquered Chittagong streets.

Jhol Momo with Broth

Steamed momos swimming in a smoky, spicy, sesame-tomato broth that warms you from the inside out. Sip the tangy, nutty, chili-kissed jhol between each dumpling. It’s soup, dumpling and pure comfort in one bowl — the kind of dish that makes rainy nights in Chittagong feel magical.
